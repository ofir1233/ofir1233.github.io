Hear's new brand puts the message inside the picture

Hear is an AI platform that reads every call a company's contact center takes. I gave it a new identity on one idea: the line is part of the painted scene, never a caption on top.

For
Strangers meeting Hear for the first time: in the feed, on the site, on a station wall.
The problem
One brand for screens and print, where a single picture tells a stranger what Hear does.
What I did
The mark, nine colors, one typeface and a painted city. I designed and built the site.
Where it stands
The site is on staging, before release. The campaign, the sign and the tote are mockups.

The idea

Hear hears every call, not a sample, and the painting shows it

Hear's case in one line: "A sample hears 2%. Hear hears 100%." Checking a sample of calls means hearing only a small share of them; Hear's AI reads all of them. The post for that line doesn't caption the claim. It paints it.

The system

One family of colors and one typeface make a screen and a painting look related

The same values run through the product and the site. In the paintings the colors are oil paint, not fixed values, but they are the same family: a blue sky, coral, lilac and green.

Color

  • One accent per screen. A page is not improved by more colors and tags.
  • Coral is a signature, used sparingly, never as a background.
  • A painted sky before a flat fill of brand color.

Type: one typeface, Byrd

  • Sentence case. No headline is set in capitals.
  • Our typeface inside the picture. The object never picks the font, and the writing is large.
  • Tight headlines; running text keeps normal spacing.

The key decision

The words are painted into the scene, never pasted on top

The same newspaper page, twice. Drag the line across the headline and watch the print change.

Rejected

Print that looked pasted

Smooth, sharp type on a painted page reads as a sticker.

Pasted on top Painted into the scene
Approved

The same page, repainted

The ink is painted with the knife, and the lines bend with the paper.

Why: the whole idea is that the world writes the message: a receipt, a plane's trail, balloon letters, a headline. Words that look laid on top read as a sticker, and the idea is lost.

Same page, same words: pasted on, it reads as a sticker; painted in, it reads as Hear.

Where it stands: I designed the brand, and designed and built the marketing site, which runs on staging. The LinkedIn campaign, the station sign and the tote are mockups; the campaign has not been published.
