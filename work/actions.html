Case study · Actions

Findings become work a person owns

The challenge
Hear, an AI platform for contact centers, raises findings through alerts, signals and evaluations. Each needs an owner and an outcome.
What I designed
The Actions board, where each finding becomes a task with a source and an assignee, and AI Tasks, a coded prototype where a person reviews what the AI proposes.
In the Lab
Built around how teams really follow up: every task keeps its source, and closed work keeps its reason, so the team can learn from what it closed, not only count it.
Role
Design and build in the Lab, with Claude Code
When
Mar–Oct 2026 · screens from Oct
Scope

Shown with

01 · The problem

A finding isn't finished until someone owns it

I designed Actions so every task shows who has it, or says “Not assigned” when nobody does yet.

Read moreShow less

Alerts fire when a monitor trips. Signals classify calls, and agent evaluations score each contact-center agent. Those screens report that something happened, not who is dealing with it or how it ended.

02 · The board

Every card follows one order, from urgency to owner

Top to bottom, a card shows how urgent it is, where it came from, what happened and who has it.

Read moreShow less

Cards sit in four columns, Pending, In progress, Done and Cancelled, under the filter bar.

Design choice: being mentioned gets its own strip on the card, so you see you're needed before you open anything.

Filters that ask whose, which and from where

Left to right, the bar answers three questions. Whose work? Everyone, Assigned to me, Mentioned me or Not assigned. Which tasks? Assignee, Status, Priority, Labels, Date and Close reason. From where? Quick filters such as From alerts, From agent evaluation and No call attached.

On the first board, mentions were an option inside the Assignee dropdown. On the current board, Assigned to me and Mentioned me have their own control.

A quick filter fades the cards that don't match instead of removing them, so the board keeps its shape.

The board's first day

I built the first board in late March 2026 and, the same day, aligned its colors to design-system tokens.

Decision 1 · Status color, day one

What color should a task that's waiting be?

  • CoralThe first color for Pending. Coral is also Hear's brand accent.
  • Neutral graySwitched the same day, then darkened step by step.

Result: by the end of the day, Pending was a darker neutral gray, chosen “for clear visibility.” The brand color no longer marked a task that is only waiting.

03 · Closing the loop

Closed work keeps its reason

A task ends in the Done or Cancelled column, and either way it says why: Resolved, Duplicate, Not actionable or Escalated.

Read moreShow less

A duplicate points to the task it repeats: "Same incident as … Closed as a duplicate." Feedback about the website search closes as Not actionable, with a line that says it isn't a contact-center issue.

Inside a task, changes stay on the record

A card opens into a side panel, and its activity log records each change, such as Created, Assigned to and Moved to In progress.

Open call and Ask AI sit at the top of the panel, so the call linked to the task is one click away.

Design choice: every task keeps its source and, once closed, its reason, so the team can see what each source raised and how it ended.

04 · AI Tasks · Earlier work · June 2026

AI Tasks: the AI proposes and a person decides

In AI Tasks, a coded prototype in the Lab, a person chooses Approve, Modify or Decline for each group of proposed changes.

Read moreShow less

Actions starts from findings. AI Tasks starts from the AI's own proposals, such as an operation to run or a change to the setup.

Three modes share one page

Requests come in three modes: Operation Requests, System Improvements and Scheduled Tasks, the last a read-only list. The page's own copy reads “AI-proposed setup changes waiting for your review.”

On one side is what's waiting. On the other is a log of past decisions.

One decision per group

A request opens in a review drawer. For each group of changes, it shows:

  • counts of what will be created, updated and deleted
  • each field's old value, struck through, beside the new one
  • the AI's reasoning for that group
  • Approve, Modify or Decline

Automatic operations sit in their own list, in plain view, under “These operations do not require approval.” The Submit decisions button stays off until every group has a decision.

Decision 2 · AI Tasks, lo-fi vs. build

What should lead the page: status, or the kind of request?

  • Status first: the lo-fi, "Mission Control"Three view tabs, then a page-wide strip of four counts (Awaiting Approval, Approved, Completed, Rejected) above two panels.
  • Kind of request first: the build, “AI Tasks”The mode is the main axis, and switching it re-slices the list, the log and the counts together.

Result: one switch sets the whole page. Status counts became four small filter cards inside the log, next to past decisions.

Decision 3 · AI Tasks, what a proposal shows

Should each proposal carry a risk rating?

  • A risk ratingTried in an earlier version of the page.
  • No ratingThe person judges the change itself: the old and new values, and the AI's reasoning.

Result: I removed the rating because the Lab's screens stay close to what Hear has. The commit message calls it a “non-real 'risk' concept.”

05 · Alerts · Earlier work · June to July 2026

Alerts: rebuilt around a data table

Alerts are one source of Actions tasks, and the filter panel from my rebuild replaced the Data page's own filter strip the same day.

Read moreShow less

The first build followed a low-fidelity wireframe almost one to one, and I rebuilt the page a few days later.

Decision 4 · Alerts, first build vs. rebuild

Keep the first build's extras, or rebuild around a data table?

  • Keep the first buildCompare mode, filter chips, a last-7-days sparkline on every row, KPI deltas and multi-select with bulk actions.
  • RebuildA data table, a stacked chart in the design system's colors, a chart that expands, count-up stat cards, a limit on active monitors and a filter panel other pages can share.

Result: the rebuild replaced the first build, without those extras.
