Case study · Actions

Every finding from a call gets an owner and an outcome

Hear's AI reads contact-center calls and raises findings. I designed Actions, the board where each finding becomes a task someone owns and closes with a reason.

For
The contact-center team that follows up on what Hear's AI finds in its calls.
The problem
A finding says what happened, not who is on it or how it ended.
What I did
Designed and built the Actions board, and the task panel each card opens.
Where it stands
Mar–Oct 2026, in Hear Design Lab, my working copy of Hear, built in code.
The real screen, with demo data.

The setting

Between a flagged call and a fixed problem, someone has to pick it up

Hear is an AI platform for contact centers. Its AI finds problems on calls; Actions turns each one into work for a person.

  1. CallsCustomers call; agents, the people who answer the calls, take them all day.
  2. Hear's AI flags somethingAlerts: checks the team sets, which fire on a call. Agent evaluations: scores for the person who took the call.
  3. My screensA task on the boardWith its source, a priority, the line from the call and an owner.
  4. A person closes itIn Done or Cancelled, with a reason the team can look back on.

The problem

A finding says something happened, not who is dealing with it

Before · a finding alone

Alert · Refund refused

The Oct 13 refund call raised an alert. It reports what happened. No name is on it, and nothing says if it was handled.

After · the same finding as a task

Evidence
02:41 “…a refund, not a voucher.”
Owner
Daniel Brooks · High priority

Three questions the person working the board brings

  1. Why is this on my board?The card names its source and quotes the call.1In the open task: where it came from
  2. What do I do about it?The task opens with its call and a checklist.3In the open task: the checklist
  3. How did it end?A closed task keeps what was done, and why.4In the open task: the outcome

Open the task

Open a card, and the task holds everything needed to finish it

This is the refund task, open beside the board. One panel says who has it, where it came from, what to do and how it ends, and keeps the team's thread on the task itself.

The card closes it too.
  1. 1Where it came from: the Refund refused alert, and the call's two asks.
  2. 2Who has it: Daniel Brooks. Reassign hands it on.
  3. 3What to do: a checklist, all three steps ticked.
  4. 4How it ends: the outcome, written in, then Close as Resolved.
  5. 5The team's thread: @ a teammate, or @AI, on the task.

Close reasons

Closed work keeps its reason, so the team can learn from it

Done or Cancelled, a task says why it ended, so the team can look back at what became of each finding.

  • ResolvedRefunded · coaching set for Oct 15.
  • EscalatedHanded on, with what came back: Ground team found the bag, Oct 6.
  • DuplicatePoints to the task it repeats: Same failure as the Oct 10 alert.
  • Not actionableFeedback on slow website search: Not about a call.

The key decision

Being mentioned gets its own filter, so you find where you're needed first

Inside the Assignee menu Set aside

On the first board, mentions were one option in the Assignee dropdown, out of sight until opened.

Its own filter, Mentions me Chosen

Beside Everyone and Assigned to me, at the start of the filter bar.

Why: a mention means someone needs you on that task, as Alex Ward is needed in the refund task's thread. Mentions me shows those tasks before you open anything.

A refused refund: one owner, three steps, one reason it closed.

Where it stands: the board and its task panel run in the Lab, on demo data; the screens are from October 2026. My part: I designed and built both with Claude Code, from the first board in late March 2026.
