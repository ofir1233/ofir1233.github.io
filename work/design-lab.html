Case study · Hear Design Lab

A working replica of Hear, built to test designs

The challenge
Hear is an AI platform for contact centers. A design has to hold up with each customer's data and volume.
What I designed
I built Hear Design Lab from scratch, on my own initiative. It's connected to an AI API, so it gives realistic answers.
Where it stands
As of October 2026, the Lab has demo environments, version switches and a demos engine. Demo mode is in development.

01 · The problem

A static mockup shows only one tidy state

So I test designs in the Lab, with real interactions and realistic data, before development.

Read moreShow less

What's in the Lab

  • The sidebar groups the screens as Main, Intelligence and Operations.
  • A workspace for each demo environment, with the customer's logo next to "Powered by Hear".
  • Three items tagged DEV at the foot of the sidebar: News · Directions, Demos and Storybook.
  • Dark and light themes.

  1. Feb 2026 · A gallery firstA separate component-gallery site. I stopped after two days.

Decision 1 · Where an alternative design sits

Should a new design get its own page, or a switch on the screen?

  • Its own page in the navThe earlier pattern was a copy of the page with its own nav item, such as Data and Data V2.
  • A Version switch on the screenFive key screens now carry the switch on the screen itself. The design changes; the data and the view don't.

Result: each version is seen on the same data and in the same view, like the three Topics designs above.

03 · Decisions

Two choices that shaped the Lab

I chose a working replica over a component gallery, and Storybook over an in-app drawer for documenting components.

Read moreShow less
Decision 2 · Gallery or replica

Should the Lab be a component gallery, or a working replica of Hear?

  • A component gallery siteMy first attempt was a separate site with a sign-in and a gallery that showed each component in Preview and Code tabs. It held two sample components when I stopped.
  • A working replica of HearThe product with its sign-in, navigation and demo data.

Result: I judge a design inside the screen it belongs to, with the data it has to carry.

Decision 3 · Where components are documented

Document components inside the app, or in Storybook?

  • The in-app Design InspectorI built it first and kept it in sync by hand.
  • StorybookI built its stories, including a Pages tier for whole screens.

Result: I kept Storybook, which renders the real components, and removed the Inspector.

Decision 4 · Two modes

Should experiments get a shell of their own?

  • One shell for everythingKeep adding every new page to the one shell of my first version.
  • A Lab shell beside itI built a separate Lab shell from the first one, for every experiment, and kept the first shell as Demo mode, with a smaller set of screens.

Result: one sign-in leads to both modes. New work lands in the Lab first, and some of it moves on to Demo mode later.

The engine that plays guided demos in the Lab has its own case study: Demos engine.

05 · How I build it

Prototyping in code, with Claude Code

I build the Lab in React, so a design review looks at a working screen, not a picture of one.

Read moreShow less

I'm a designer with a background in coding.

Three patterns run through the work.

  1. Duplicate, compare, deleteA new idea gets its own copy of the screen while the original keeps working. Later one of them goes: News V1 was dropped, and V2 became the News tab. Data still keeps two versions behind one switch.
  2. Real fields, illustrative valuesScreens stay close to what Hear has. News uses real field and metric names with illustrative values, and Data's filter presets point only at columns that can be filtered. I also took out an invented “risk” concept.
  3. Reuse before inventingI build new pages from parts the Lab already has. The Settings section uses only existing components, and the Knowledge glossary row reuses the item row from Signals.
