Case study · Demos engine

Sales demos that drive the Design Lab from a script

The challenge
A generic deck tells every prospect the same story. Slides can describe Hear; they can't show it happening.
What I designed
A demos engine that drives the Design Lab's working screens from a script: it types the prospect's questions into the Lab's chat, the Lab answers on demo data, and a camera moves across the screens.
In the Lab
Each sequence plays in the Lab during a meeting or opens in a clean tab to record as a video, in English or Hebrew, some with narration.

01 · The problem

A generic deck tells every prospect the same story

I wanted a prospect to watch the screens answer the questions they would ask about their own business.

Read moreShow less

Hear answers questions about a contact center's calls, chats and tickets. Slides can describe that. They can't show it happening.

The engine lives in the Design Lab, my working replica of Hear, which I built from scratch. Each customer gets its own demo environment there.

02 · Setup

A new demo starts from a short form

A new environment starts from Set up your demo.

Read moreShow less

It asks for three things: the company's website, a few sentences that describe the company, and a file (PDF, CSV, TXT or DOCX). Then Start demo.

The form speaks to the company directly: Your company website URL, Describe your company in a few sentences. A link under the button leads back to the list of environments.

Decision 1 · One demo or many

One generic deck, or a demo built for each prospect?

  • One generic deckThe same slides for every prospect, whatever their business.
  • A demo for each prospectIts own demo environment in the Lab, and sequences that tell its story on the Lab's working screens.

Result: the Lab keeps a demo environment for each customer, and the library holds sequences written around one company or industry, like an insurer's renewals or a leasing company's calls.

03 · The engine

A script that drives the Lab's working screens

A sequence is not a screen recording: it drives the Lab itself.

Read moreShow less

It types into the real composer, presses the real send key, and moves a camera over the real screens. The library says so in its own header.

  1. Shows a claimA full-screen title card with a single claim, between the product's own screens.
  2. Types the questionInto the real composer, letter by letter, with the AI suggestions showing underneath.
  3. Presses sendThe real send key. The product thinks, then answers on demo data.
  4. Moves the cameraPushes in on the answer, then dims the screen and lifts out one table.
  5. SpeaksNarration in English or Hebrew. Voices have their own tab in the library.
Decision 2 · Where demos run

A separate demo shell, or the Lab I design in?

  • A separate demo shellIn July, the first version's Demo mode was an older layout of the product. Several pages showed Coming soon, and only some new work reached it.
  • Drive the Lab itselfSequences run on the Lab's own screens, the same ones I test designs on, with the same demo data.

Result: the showcase above plays on the Lab's own Chat screen and sidebar, and Demos sits in that same sidebar.

04 · The library

Each demo is a short story with a running time

Showcase sequences sit in one library inside the Lab, and each card is a ready demo.

Read moreShow less

New sequence starts another. Tabs hold the Archive, Blocks, Voices and Docs, and the list sorts by default order or by Top rated.

What a card holds

  • A title and its running time
  • An id and a step count
  • One line that says what the story is
  • Tags for the language, the kind of story (Recap, Daily, Briefing, Capabilities), Narrated, Film, Scored and an approximate length
  • A star rating, and the list can sort by Top rated
  • Play, Clean run and Copy link, plus archive and delete

Two details

Languages as siblings: the Hebrew July recap shares the English one's id (plus a language suffix), its step count and its running time, and its card says "same slides."

A story shape: two cards describe themselves as "one claim, two questions, thirty seconds." The showcase at the top of this page follows that shape: a title card, then two questions.
