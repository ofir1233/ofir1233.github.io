Case study · Demos engine

Scripted demos for prospects, played inside Hear Design Lab

The challenge
Slides can describe Hear, an AI platform for contact centers, but they can't show it working.
What I designed
A demos engine in the Lab: a script types questions, the Lab answers on demo data, and a camera moves across the screens. Sequences come in English or Hebrew.

The file can be a PDF, CSV, TXT or DOCX.

Decision 1 · One demo or many

One deck for everyone, or a demo for each prospect?

  • One deck for everyoneThe same slides, whatever the prospect's business.
  • A demo for each prospectIts own demo environment, and sequences that tell its story on the Lab's screens.

Result: each customer or prospect has a demo environment in the Lab. The library holds sequences written around one company or industry, like an insurer's renewals or a leasing company's calls.

03 · The engine

A script that drives the Lab's working screens

I chose to run sequences in the Lab itself, rather than in a separate demo shell.

Read moreShow less
  1. Shows a claimA full-screen title card with a single claim, between the Lab's screens.
  2. Types the questionInto the real composer, letter by letter, with the AI suggestions showing underneath.
  3. Presses sendThe real send key. The Lab shows its thinking state, then answers.
  4. Moves the cameraPushes in on the answer, then dims the screen and lifts out one table.
  5. SpeaksSome sequences add narration in English or Hebrew.
Decision 2 · Where demos run

A separate demo shell, or the Lab I design in?

  • A separate demo shellDemo mode, the Lab's first shell, had an older layout in July. Several pages showed Coming soon, and only some new work reached it.
  • Drive the Lab itselfSequences run on the Lab's own screens, the same ones I test designs on, with the same demo data.

Result: the showcase above plays on the Lab's own Chat home and sidebar.

04 · The library

Each demo is a short story with a running time

Showcase sequences sit in one library, and each card is a ready demo.

Read moreShow less

Tabs hold the Archive, Blocks, Voices and Docs, and the New sequence button starts a new one.

What a card holds

  • A title and its running time
  • An id and a step count
  • One line that says what the story is
  • Tags for the language, the kind of story (Recap, Daily, Briefing, Capabilities), Narrated, Film, Scored and an approximate length
  • A star rating, and the list can sort by Top rated
  • Play, Clean run and Copy link, plus archive and delete

The showcase at the top of this page follows the “one claim, two questions, thirty seconds” shape that two cards describe.
