Case study · Newsroom

An AI news feed that says why each story is there

The challenge
An AI headline saying a topic surged, with no baseline, time window or calls, asks people to trust the wording.
What I designed
Newsroom, a news feed for Hear, an AI platform for contact centers. Each story shows its source, how strong it is and the calls it rests on.
Where it stands
The News tab in Hear Design Lab, a working replica of Hear. It also runs in Hebrew, right to left.
Role
Design and build in the Lab, with Claude Code

01 · The problem

AI summaries shouldn't sound more certain than their data

I put each story's evidence on the page: its source, its time window, a baseline for any spike and its calls.

Read moreShow less

An AI summary of a contact center is only worth reading if people can check it.

  1. Labeled data. The header says "feed · illustrative data" on the page itself, not in a footnote.
  2. Just detected. Each detection scrolls past with the clock time it happened.
  3. Monthly recap. The month as a 75-second full-screen story with sound, which starts when you press Play.
  4. Sources. The reports behind the stories, with counts and dates, listed apart from live detections. Picking one filters the feed.
  5. Baseline. A spike is drawn against a dotted baseline, with the peak marked.
  6. Time window. A trend is labeled “this week” and claims no timestamp it doesn't have.
  7. Evidence. Each story ends with what it rests on, such as a “See N calls” line.

Design choice: a story distilled from a report names that report and the dates it covers, so a reader can trace the claim to its source.

From editorial events to stories tied to the platform

Decision 1 · What counts as a story

Should stories be editorial events, or things people can open in Hear?

  • Editorial events (V1)General events about the contact center, shown with charts from a widget library.
  • Real platform objects (V2)Every story comes from a real part of Hear, such as a signal, an evaluation or a report. Names of fields and metrics are real; the values are illustrative.

Why: every story should point at something real in Hear, an event “tied to a real platform surface.”

Decision 2 · Where actions sit

Should every card in the feed carry its own action buttons?

  • Buttons on every cardEdit signal, Open in Data and Read story on each card in the feed.
  • Actions on the article pageThe whole card opens the story. Edit signal, Open in Data and Run report sit there, with the "why" panel and the sources.

Result: I put the actions on the article page, and the feed still works that way. Each card ends in its evidence line, with no action buttons.

03 · Showing evidence

Precision only where the platform has it

In each story's “Why you're seeing this” line, I show a threshold only where a configured rule sets one, so trends stay in words.

Read moreShow less

V1's lead story put a precise percentage in its headline. In the trust layer I defined the rules in the feed's data, one set per story type.

Decision 3 · How exact to sound

How much precision should a story's trigger show?

  • Numbers everywhereEvery trigger carries a figure, even for trends that have no configured threshold.
  • Precision by story typeTrends get a trigger in words and a time window. Alerts, compliance and evaluations show their real configured thresholds.

Why: a trend has no configured threshold, so any number in its trigger would be made up. Trend triggers stay qualitative, with “no invented numeric thresholds.”

Every story gets a strength label (strong, moderate or for watch), itself marked illustrative, and a “See N calls” line. The article chat gets the article as context and is told that its figures are illustrative.

How the charts carry it

  • Eleven custom SVG chart types, from trend lines to a causal chain, sit in one registry. A story names the chart it needs, and the registry draws it.
  • Articles pair each chart with a stats grid and a data table, so the figures also exist as text.
  • Story text is set in a serif, a deliberate exception to the design system, scoped to News.
04 · Directions & Hebrew

Four ways to frame the same news

A week after the built feed, I sketched four low-fidelity directions, each with a different main metaphor.

Read moreShow less

I kept them at wireframe fidelity on purpose, as gray skeletons with notes. They sit in the Lab's sidebar as News · Directions, tagged DEV.

  1. 01Editionread what changedA front page, like the built feed.
  2. 02Radarsee the whole operation at once
  3. 03Inboxwork the queue
  4. 04Briefingtell me, then let me dig

Every direction keeps the same three parts in its side panel: why, evidence and actions. All four remain explorations.

Hebrew, right to left

Then News went to Hebrew: about 20 articles were translated, the News area switched to right to left and the article chat answered in Hebrew. In July, News was the only tab in the Lab that changed language.

The app shell around News stayed in English. Numbers stay in Latin digits, as is standard in Hebrew interfaces.

05 · Where AI belongs

In the chat input, I added a moving logo, then took it out

In April and May I reworked parts of the chat home; some of what I added around the input, I later took out again.

Read moreShow less

News puts the AI's stories at the front of the page. The chat home is a different case, one I built in the Lab's first version.

  1. SuggestionsAI suggestions capped at five, with the original suggestions design restored.
  2. Daily briefingTried inside the suggestions, then back in the page. It now fades out while you chat.
  3. ControlsA slash-command button added. The plus and attachment button removed.
Decision 4 · The AI's presence

Does the AI need a face inside the input?

  • Logo in the fieldA morphing logo and a dot ring, with the logo in the input reacting to each keystroke.
  • A plain fieldThe logo leaves the input. The field holds what you type, two shortcut buttons and a mic.

Result: the Hear mark greets you above the field, and the field is left for the question.
