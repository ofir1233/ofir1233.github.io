Case study · Newsroom

AI stories a reader can check against the calls

Hear's AI reads every call in a contact center. Newsroom, the news feed I designed for it, writes what changed as short stories, and each story shows the evidence behind it.

For
The people who read what Hear finds in their contact center's calls, and act on it.
The problem
An AI headline can sound more certain than its data, and asks people to trust the wording.
What I did
Designed and built Newsroom: a front page of stories, an article page for each, and a rule for how exact a story may sound.
Where it stands
Built in July 2026 and updated since, as the News tab in the Lab, my working copy of Hear, with demo data.
The real News screen, with demo data. Open any story.

The setting

Four parts of Hear raise findings; one front page gathers them

Newsroom turns those findings into stories on one front page, with a tab for each source. It also holds a monthly recap, the month as a 75-second story that plays only when you press Play.

  1. CallsAgents, the people who answer customer calls, take calls all day.
  2. Hear's AI reads every callSignals (classifiers that run on calls), agent evaluations, compliance checks and alerts each raise findings.
  3. My screensA front page of storiesWhat changed in the calls, written as stories you can read or play.
  4. A person checks, then actsEach story opens to an article page with its sources and actions, such as Open in Data.

The problem

An AI line can sound more certain than its data

A summary of a contact center is only worth reading if people can check it. So each story carries its own evidence, and the reader can test the headline against it.

What counts as a story

Every story points at something real you can open in Hear

General events Set aside

Events about the contact center in general, each with a chart. Nothing to open behind them.

Things in Hear Chosen

Each story comes from a real part of Hear, such as a signal, an evaluation or a report. A story drawn from a report names it and the dates it covers.

Why: a reader can follow any story back to its source. It also means a card in the feed needs no buttons: the whole card opens the story's article page, where its actions sit, such as Edit signal and Open in Data.

A week after the feed was built, I also sketched four other ways to frame the same news, from a front page to a work queue; they remain low-fidelity sketches.

The key decision

No invented numbers: a story sounds only as exact as its data

Nobody set a threshold for cancellation intent; like every trend, the story measures a change over time. So it says “3.4 times its usual day”, against its own baseline, never that it passed a number no one chose. Alerts, compliance checks and evaluations have a threshold someone set, so their stories name it.

A number on every trigger Set aside

Every story names a threshold it crossed, even a trend that has none. That figure would be made up, and a reader could not check it.

As exact as the data Chosen

Trends
The trigger in words, against a usual day; time as a window, such as “this week”
Alerts, compliance, evaluations
The real threshold that was set; a clock time for a real moment, such as an alert
Every story
The method behind it, and a line that leads to its calls

Why: the feed is worth reading only if a reader can check it, and a number nobody set cannot be checked.
