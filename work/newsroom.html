01 · The problem

A summary that sounds surer than its data misleads

An AI summary of a contact center is only worth reading if people can check it. A headline that says a topic surged, with no baseline, no time frame and no calls behind it, asks the team to trust the wording.

So each story in Newsroom carries its evidence on the page: the source it came from, the time window it covers, a spike drawn against its baseline, and a closing line such as "See N calls".

  1. Labeled data. The header says "feed · illustrative data" on the page itself, not in a footnote.
  2. Just detected. Each detection scrolls past with the clock time it happened.
  3. Monthly recap. The month as a 75-second full-screen story with sound, which starts when you press Play.
  4. Sources. Reports the stories were distilled from, with counts and dates, kept apart from detections that come from no report. Pick one to filter the feed.
  5. Baseline. A spike is drawn against a dotted baseline, with the peak marked.
  6. Time window. A trend says "this week". It doesn't claim a timestamp it doesn't have.
  7. Evidence. Each story ends with what it rests on, such as "See N calls".

Design choice: a story distilled from a report names that report and the dates it covers, so a reader can trace the claim back to where it came from.

02 · How it evolved

From editorial events to stories tied to the platform

Newsroom lives in the Lab's News tab, which took its shape in July 2026. I built V2 as a copy of V1 and left the original untouched beside it. Four days later V1 was gone, and V2 became the News tab.

  1. 2 Jul · News V1An editorial event feed: highlights, a KPI strip, trending topics, a compliance watch, a timeline and a library of chart widgets. The lead story ended in "Create Signal".
  2. 2 Jul · News V2A copy of V1 where every event comes from a real part of the platform: Signals, Monitoring, AI Tasks, Compliance, Agent evaluation, Reports, Workflows, Customers and the Daily briefing.
  3. 6 Jul · Front pageA masthead, a breaking strip, a lead story, sections by platform area, and an article page for each story. V1 deleted.
  4. 6 Jul · Trust layer"Why you're seeing this" on every story, a "See N calls" evidence line, and a chat inside the article that answers about it, on the chat backend from a colleague's starting app.
  5. 13 Jul · Four directionsLow-fidelity wireframes of four other ways to frame the same news. Chapter 04.
  6. 13 Jul · HebrewThe News tab in Hebrew, right to left, with its own scope rules. Chapter 04.
Decision 1 · What counts as a story

Should the feed report editorial events, or only things a user can open in the product?

  • Editorial events (V1)General editorial events about the contact center, shown with charts from a widget library.
  • Real platform objects (V2)Every story comes from a real part of Hear, such as a signal, a monitor, an AI task, a compliance score, an evaluation or a report. Names of fields and metrics are real; the values are illustrative.

Why: so every story points at something real in Hear, an event "tied to a real platform surface". V1 was deleted four days later.

Decision 2 · Where actions sit

Should every card in the feed carry its own action buttons?

  • Buttons on every cardEdit signal, Open in Data and Read story on the cards themselves, in the feed.
  • Actions on the article pageThe whole card opens the story. Edit signal, Open in Data and Run report sit there, with the "why" panel and the sources.

Result: the whole card opens the article, and actions live on the article page. The current feed still works this way: story cards end in their evidence line and carry no action buttons.

03 · Showing evidence

Precision only where the platform has it

V1's lead story put a precise percentage in its headline. In the trust layer I wrote the rules into the feed's data, one set per story type. Each story's "Why you're seeing this" line shows its trigger, its basis and how strong the signal is.

Decision 3 · How exact to sound

How much precision should a story's trigger show?

  • Numbers everywhereEvery trigger carries a figure, as V1's lead story did, even for trends that have no configured threshold.
  • Precision by story typeTrends get a trigger in words and a time window. Alerts, compliance and evaluations show their real configured thresholds, and real moments, such as alerts, keep their clock times.

Why: a trend has no configured threshold, so any number in its trigger would be made up. Trend triggers stay qualitative, with "no invented numeric thresholds", and only real moments, such as calls and alerts, carry clock times.

Every story gets a strength label (strong, moderate or for watch), itself marked illustrative in the Lab, and a "See N calls" line for its evidence. The article chat gets the article as context and is told that its figures are illustrative.

How the charts carry it

  • Eleven hand-built SVG chart types sit in one registry, from trend lines and gauges to a causal chain, so a story names the chart it needs and the registry draws it.
  • Each chart measures its container, so the SVG fills the width at real pixel size. Every color is a CSS variable, so light and dark flip with no extra code.
  • Articles pair each chart with a stats grid and a data table, so the figures also exist as text.
  • Story text is set in a serif, a deliberate exception to the design system, scoped to News.
04 · Directions & Hebrew

Four ways to frame the same news

A week after the built feed, I sketched four low-fidelity directions, each a different main metaphor over the same content: grey skeletons with notes, wireframe fidelity on purpose. They sit in the Lab's sidebar as News · Directions, tagged DEV.

  1. 01Editionread what changedA front page, like the built feed.
  2. 02Radarsee the whole operation at once
  3. 03Inboxwork the queueMarked as best for the risk owner.
  4. 04Briefingtell me, then let me dig

What stays: every direction keeps the same three parts in its side panel: why, evidence, actions. All four remain explorations.

Hebrew, right to left

Then News went to Hebrew: about 20 articles translated, the News area switches to right to left, and the chat inside an article answers in Hebrew. In July, News was the only tab in the Lab that changed language.

The scope is set in one place: the app shell stays in English, and numbers stay in Latin digits, as is standard in Hebrew interfaces. Borders, quotes, arrows and padding use logical CSS properties, so one set of styles serves both directions, and arrows flip.

05 · Where AI belongs

In the chat input, I added a moving logo, then took it out

News puts the AI's stories at the front of the page. The chat home is a different case: it came with that starting app, and in April and May I reworked parts of it. Some of what I added around the input, I later took out again.

  1. SuggestionsAI suggestions capped at five, with the original suggestions design restored.
  2. Daily briefingTried inside the suggestions, then back in the page. It now fades out while you chat.
  3. ControlsA slash-command button added. The plus and attachment button removed.
Decision 4 · The AI's presence

Does the AI need a face inside the input?

  • Logo in the fieldA morphing logo and a dot ring, with the logo in the input reacting to each keystroke.
  • A plain fieldThe logo leaves the input. The field holds what you type, two shortcut buttons and a mic.

Result: the Hear mark greets you above the field, and the field is left for the question.

Where it landed

In News, the AI's stories lead the page, and each one carries its source, its time window and its calls. In Chat, the AI's mark sits outside the field, and the briefing steps aside while you chat.
