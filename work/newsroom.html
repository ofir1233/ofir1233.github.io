  1. 01

    Four parts of Hear, one front page

    Four parts of Hear raise findings from calls: signals, agent evaluations, compliance checks and alerts. Newsroom turns their findings into short stories on one front page, with a tab for each source. On Tuesday, October 13, the last 7 days hold 31.

  2. 02

    An AI headline can sound more certain than the data

    On 7 Sep 2026, I checked my design against Hear’s production code. I found a customer’s AI report with a chart range: 12.7% to 13.9%. Hear stores a sentence comparing two periods, plus a yes or no for a drastic change. The range is not stored anywhere. The AI wrote it into the page.

  3. 03

    Every story shows what it is based on

    The lead story names its source and time. Its chart shows a usual day, 17 calls, as a dashed line, so the rise to 58 has something to be measured against. One sentence says why the story is here, how strong it is, and links to its 211 calls. The card has no buttons. It opens the story, where the actions live.

  4. 04

    Only as exact as the data

    A trend has no threshold anyone set, and Hear keeps its change only in words. So a trend story uses words too, over a window like this week. Alerts, compliance checks and evaluations name the real threshold, and a clock time for a real moment. The 3.4 times above is for when a trend’s numbers are stored. I wrote this rule into the News tab’s code in July 2026.

  5. 05

    The month, played as a story

    Once a month, the front page has a recap: September in 75 seconds. It starts only when you press Play. It counts 38,214 calls, 9% more than August, marks the busiest day, Monday Sep 21, and names the biggest change: refund calls doubled for one week, from 963 to 2,014, after the schedule changed.

Where it stands

Newsroom is a concept, not part of Hear’s product. It lives in the News tab in Hear Design Lab, my working copy of Hear, on demo data with real field names and illustrative values. I designed and built it with Claude Code in July 2026, moving from an editorial feed to stories tied to real parts of Hear. Then I sketched four directions (Edition, Radar, Inbox, Briefing) and ran it in Hebrew, right to left. In September I checked it against the production code.
