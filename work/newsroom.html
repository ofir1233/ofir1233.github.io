Case study · Newsroom

AI stories a reader can check against the calls

Hear's AI reads every call in a contact center. Newsroom, the news feed I designed for it, writes what changed as short stories, and each story shows the evidence behind it.

For
People who use Hear to see what changed in their contact center's calls.
The problem
An AI headline can sound more certain than its data, and asks people to trust the wording.
What I did
Designed and built Newsroom: a front page of stories, an article page for each, and a rule for how exact a story may sound.
Where it stands
Jul 2026, updated since. The News tab in Hear Design Lab, my working replica of Hear, with demo data.
The real News screen, with demo data. Open any story.

The setting

Hear already finds things in the calls; the feed says what changed

Newsroom gathers what Hear's AI finds into one front page. It also holds a monthly recap, the month as a 75-second story that plays only when you press Play.

  1. CallsAgents, the people who answer customer calls, take calls all day.
  2. Hear's AI reads every callSignals, agent evaluations, compliance checks and alerts each raise findings.
  3. My screensA front page of storiesWhat changed in the calls, written as stories you can read or play.
  4. A person checks, then actsEach story opens to an article page with its sources and actions, such as Open in Data.

The problem

An AI line can sound more certain than its data

A summary of a contact center is only worth reading if people can check it. So each story carries its own evidence: its source, its time window, a baseline for any spike, and its calls.

How it works

Three questions a reader can answer from the story itself

1 of 3

The hard rule

No invented numbers: a trend's trigger stays in words

Alerts, compliance and evaluations are measured against a threshold someone set. A trend has none, so any number in its trigger would be made up. I set how exact each kind of story may sound.

Trends

Measured over a period

Trigger
In words, with no invented threshold
Time
A window, such as “this week”
Basis
The method behind the story

Alerts, compliance, evaluations

Measured against a rule someone set up

Trigger
The real threshold that was set
Time
A clock time for a real moment, such as an alert
Basis
The method behind the story

Set aside: a figure on every trigger, even where no threshold exists. Every story also gets a strength label (strong, moderate or for watch) and a line that leads to its calls.

What counts as a story

Every story points at something real you can open in Hear

General events Set aside

Events about the contact center in general, each with a chart. Nothing to open behind them.

Things in Hear Chosen

Each story comes from a real part of Hear, such as a signal, an evaluation or a report. A story drawn from a report names it and the dates it covers.

Why: a reader can follow any story back to its source. It also means a card in the feed needs no buttons: the whole card opens the story's article page, where its actions sit, such as Edit signal, Open in Data and Run report.

Explorations

Four other ways to frame the same news, kept as sketches

A week after the feed was built, I sketched four low-fidelity directions, each around a different main idea. They are gray wireframes with notes, and remain explorations.

  1. Editionread what changed
  2. Radarsee the whole operation at once
  3. Inboxwork the queue
  4. Briefingtell me, then let me dig

Edition is a front page, like the built feed. Every direction keeps the same three parts in its side panel: why, evidence and actions.

Where it stands

Newsroom is the News tab in Hear Design Lab, my working replica of Hear, rebuilt in code with demo data. The names of fields and metrics are real; the values are illustrative. The tab also runs in Hebrew, right to left.

My part

I designed and built Newsroom in July 2026 with Claude Code, and have updated it since: the front page, the article page and the rule for how exact a story may sound.
