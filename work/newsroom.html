Case study · Newsroom

AI stories a reader can check against the calls

Hear's AI reads every call a contact center takes. Newsroom, the feed I designed for it, tells what changed as short stories.

For
People who read what Hear finds in their calls, and act on it.
The problem
An AI headline can sound more certain than its data.
What I did
Stories that show their evidence, and a rule for how exact each may sound.
Where it stands
A concept: the News tab in Hear Design Lab, my working copy of Hear, built in code; not in the product.
The real News screen, with demo data. Open any story.

The setting

Four parts of Hear raise findings; one front page gathers them

The four are signals (labels Hear puts on calls, by AI or by rule), agent evaluations (scores for the person who took the call), compliance checks, and alerts (checks the team sets, which fire on a call). Newsroom turns their findings into stories on one front page, with a tab for each source.

  1. CallsAgents, the people who answer the calls, take calls all day.
  2. Hear's AI reads every callEach of the four raises findings.
  3. My screensA front page of storiesWhat changed in the calls, written as short stories.
  4. A person checks, then actsEvery story points at something real: its article page has its sources and actions.

The problem

Hear's own AI already draws numbers nobody stored

Not a guess: checking the design against the production code (7 Sep 2026), I found a customer's AI report with a range on its chart. The AI had written it into the page; no range is stored anywhere.

The key decision

A trend says it in words until its numbers are stored

Alerts, compliance checks and agent evaluations have a threshold someone set, so their stories name it. A trend has none, and today Hear keeps its change only in words, so its story says it in words. The headline above is the version for when its numbers are stored.

A number on every trigger Set aside

Every story names a threshold it crossed, even a trend that has none: a made-up figure no reader could check.

As exact as the data Chosen

Trends
The change in words until its numbers are stored, then against a usual day; time as a window, such as “this week”
Alerts, compliance, evaluations
The real threshold that was set; a clock time for a real moment, such as an alert

Why: Hear stores a trend's change only in words, a yes-or-no “drastic change” and a sentence, so a number there today would be made up. I wrote the rule into the News tab's code in July 2026.
