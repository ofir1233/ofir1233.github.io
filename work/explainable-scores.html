Case study · Explainable scores

Every AI score leads back to the calls behind it

Hear's AI reads every call in a contact center and scores the agents, the people who answer the calls, against the center's rules. I made every score open into its evidence.

For
The people who coach agents and check compliance in a contact center.
The problem
A score with nothing behind it can only be trusted blindly or ignored.
What I did
Designed and built the grid of scores, each score's popover, and its AI answer.
Where it stands
The heatmap shipped: I built it into Hear's product in May 2026. The rest is in Hear Design Lab, my working copy of Hear.
The real screen, with demo data. Click any score.

The setting

A score here is about a person, not a machine

An agent's scores decide who gets coached, and on what.

  1. CallsAgents take customer calls all day.
  2. Hear's AI reads every callIt checks each call against the center's rules, such as fee disclosure or the vulnerable customer protocol.
  3. My screensA score per agent, per ruleOver a date range, with the calls it rests on.
  4. A person actsSomeone decides whom to coach, and on which rule.

The problem

A bare number leaves two bad options: trust it blindly, or ignore it

54

Before · a score alone

In the first version I built in the Lab, scores came over time and per skill, in tables of teams and agents. Nothing said why a score was what it was.

Three questions a coach brings to a low score

  1. Is this score really bad?Only a score under the pass mark of 70 takes color, so a low one stands out before anyone reads a number.1Answered in layer 1, the rule
  2. Is it the agent, or the whole team?The popover puts the agent's score beside the team's, against the same pass mark.2Answered in layer 2, what it rests on
  3. What actually happened on the calls?Ask AI, the AI assistant in Hear's sidebar, names the calls, what most of them share, and the step that was missed on each.3Answered in layer 3, the calls

After · pull the thread

Three clicks under one low score are the calls themselves

This is Selma's 54 from the grid. Each click opens the layer the screens open next; the layer above folds down to its answer.

  3. 3

    On 9 of the 12, the customer said they were struggling to pay, and the protocol questions were skipped.

    The popover's one button, Ask AI which calls, sends the agent and the rule with the question already written. The answer gives:

    1. 1How many: 12 calls failed.
    2. 2What most share: 9 of them, one cause.
    3. 3Each call: what happened and when the protocol was skipped, such as 01:48 into a 6 min 12 sec call on Oct 13.

    The answer names the cause before it names the person.

The key decision

A question about a score starts from the score, not from a blank chat

A blank chat Set aside

Ask AI from the top of the sidebar, which keeps the page as context. You still write the question, and say which number you mean.

The score itself Chosen

Ask AI which calls, the popover's one button, sends the agent and the rule with the question.

Why: because it carries the agent and the rule, the answer can name the calls. The sidebar stays for open questions about the page.

One low score, twelve failed calls, one cause behind nine of them.

Shipped: I built the compliance heatmap into Hear's product myself (May 2026); it is now Hear's only Compliance page, and developers later added a popover to its cells and reused the heatmap on Agent evaluation. Still a proposal: asking AI from a score, which the product does not have yet. My part: design, and the front-end code of the heatmap; the Lab screens with Claude Code, March to October 2026.
