# FYI: how the art serves the story

Read from: work/fyi.html (seven parts, five Decision blocks, token table), work/fyi-scenes.js (welcome and intro copy, demo story), and the four review sheets (pepper, studio-session, neon-styles, neon-grown-up). Nothing in the repo was changed.

---

## 1. The idea

**One sentence.** FYI is the glass a rough idea passes through on its way to an audience: silent while the crew works, loud when the work meets the world.

**The narrative, five lines.**

1. **Who.** People who make things for a living, in small crews: the artist, the director, the designer, the one who knows a room. Adults who are good at what they do.
2. **The promise.** Say the rough version out loud at 11:14 PM. Thirty-nine days later there are doors, a time and people in the room, and nothing got lost on the way.
3. **How it keeps it.** Each step makes the idea sharper without retyping it: voice note, transcript, plan, tasks, event, public. Every later thing points back to the message it came from.
4. **The name.** "Focus" is the mechanic, not a slogan. The idea starts wide and soft and ends as one sharp thing with a date on it.
5. **What the art is for.** To show the one thing the interface cannot: the room. The heat, the people absorbed in work, the warmth of being in it together. The UI is the tool on the desk; the art is why you picked it up.

**The two halves of the look already say this.** The look he chose has two ingredients, and they map to the two ends of the product:

- **The lens** (extreme wide angle, pushed close) is the moment of capture. Everything is in the frame and nothing is sorted yet. That is the voice note.
- **The print** (halftone, ink line, cyan and magenta plates slightly off) is the moment of publication. That is the poster.

So the art has a direction of travel: **paint becomes print, the way a voice note becomes a night.** And "focus" has a visible form: two offset plates sliding into register. That single move is the brand's signature, and it is rationed (section 2).

## 2. The jobs of the art

**The glass rule.** The art is always the world *outside* the phone, looking at it or seen from it. The interface is what is *inside*. They do not mix. Everything below follows from that.

| Job | What it is | Allowed | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The room** | Painted scenes of the studio, always shot from where the phone lies (desk, piano lid, flight case, later the venue bar). People absorbed in work, never posing for us. | App Store, website, the case-study page around the phones, welcome and the three intro pages, a first-run empty state. | Behind messages, as chat wallpaper, behind any text that has to be read, inside or beside an AI card, in settings. |
| **The witness** | The cat (section 3). | A counted handful of places, all outside the glass. | The thread, AI, tasks, calls, notifications, loading states. |
| **The press** | The print finish as a material: halftone, plates, registration, ink line. | Only on things the crew publishes: the event poster, Story, Post, Link page, a project cover. | Chrome. No halftone on buttons, no brush-stroke dividers, no textured bubbles, no painted icons. |
| **The hand** | Stickers and rough marker lines. | Only as something a *person* does: a vote stuck on cover B, a reaction, the marker Ines uses to mark the arcs on Theo's cut. | As decoration the brand adds. No sticker confetti, no graffiti headings, no tags in the tab bar. |

Two consequences worth stating:

- **Graffiti gets a function.** Inside the product, the rough marker line is the annotation stroke. It exists because a person marked up someone's work. That is how it stays grown-up.
- **The brand lends its technique, not its palette.** A crew's poster is printed in the crew's colour (Copper Hours is copper). Pink is FYI's colour, not every user's. Otherwise every launch on the platform looks the same.

**The waveform is the thread.** In the art, the only thing ever shown on a phone screen is one pink waveform. Never a painted UI. In the product, the launch poster's graphic can be built from the first voice note's 30 bars. The poster is the voice note, printed. It fits the page's own decision that formats are views, not copies.

### The loudness ladder

**Principle: the art gets louder as the audience gets bigger.** One person, silent. The crew, quiet. The public, loud.

| Level | Where | What is there | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 Silent** | AI catch-up and every AI card, the working thread, composer, tasks, calls, message info, settings | True black. Role colours only. No art, no cat, no texture. | "Only you see this" is the smallest audience there is. And nobody should have to guess whether a person or a model said something, so nothing with a face goes near the violet hairline. |
| **1 Whisper** | Chat list, Projects list, a vote on a cover | One small painted thumbnail per row at most (the project cover). A sticker a person placed. | The crew's own things carry the colour. |
| **2 Quiet** | Welcome, intro pages, a tab's first-run empty state | One picture in the top half, one line, one button. Gone once there is data. | First run is the only time the product may speak for itself. |
| **3 Clear** | The event card in the thread, the formats sheet, the Project cover | The press material, framed as an object. | Something is about to leave the group. |
| **4 Loud** | The Public switch, Day 39 "Live now", App Store, website, the case-study hero | Full colour, full bleed, and the register snap. | The audience is everyone. |

**The register snap happens in two places only:** the mark on launch, and the moment an event goes public. Candidate third use, to test rather than assume: a format that is "Out of date" shows its plates slightly off until Refresh puts them back. It always keeps its text label.

## 3. The cat

**Decision: the cat is not the AI. It is the first audience.**

The page already decided that AI is a layer with no avatar, because a face makes a model look like a member. A cat that summarises threads is Clippy with whiskers, and it would undo the product's best decision. So the opposite: the cat understands nothing. It is the one creature in the room that is curious about what is inside the phone. That curiosity is what every creator is trying to earn from strangers. The cat is the audience before there is an audience.

**Character.** Curious, unimpressed, patient, slightly in the way. It lives in the studio. The crew ignores it and it ignores them. It is interested in exactly one thing: the glass.

**How it behaves.** Like a real cat. It looks, paws, sits on the thing you need, sleeps on the flight case, leaves.

**What it never does.** Speaks. Has a speech bubble. Points at the interface. Waves. Wears anything. Celebrates. Shows a set of emotions. Appears as a spinner, an avatar or a notification icon.

**Where it appears (five places, counted).** The hero image. Welcome. One first-run empty state (asleep on a closed flight case). Offline (sitting on the cable). The Day 39 picture. Everywhere else: no cat.

**Name.** It has one the way a studio cat has one: the crew uses it once in the demo thread. The product and the brand never say it. No "Pepper says". (The review file is called pepper.jpg; if that is the intended name, it works. I did not treat it as decided.)

**The risk, and the guardrail.** The childish reading he already rejected comes from character design, not from having an animal. The rule: **the lens exaggerates, the design does not.** Real cat anatomy, real fur, real eye size; the wide lens makes it big because it is close. The current q2 draft is a notch too far toward glossy cartoon eyes and should come back toward a real animal. Also: the humans in the frame are never looking at the cat, and the acid yellow, crowns, stars, bolts and drips from round 2 stay out. Those were the childish signals, not the pink.

**The dog: not a character.** Two animals make a cast, and a cast makes a cartoon. The dog in headphones is also the one costume in the set. Keep the picture he loved as a single image outside the product (the "listening together" picture for the website or the Together part of the page). It is somebody's dog in the room. It gets no role, no second pose, and it never shares a frame with the cat as a pair.

## 4. Voice

Plain, specific, counted. It names people and times. It is warm through precision, not through adjectives. The AI's lines are flatter than everyone else's and are never playful.

**Right**

- Empty chat: "Say the rough version. That's the first message."
- AI catch-up: "While you were away: 64 messages, 3 decisions, 1 open question. Only you see this."
- Event goes public: "It's out. Fri, Nov 20, doors 9 PM, Dock Hall 3. Anyone with the link can RSVP."
- Error: "Not sent. The 2.1 GB file is safe here and goes when you're back online."
- Notification: "Theo's cut is due tomorrow. It's waiting on your stems."

**Wrong**

- "Meow! It's quiet in here." (The cat does not speak.)
- "Your AI bestie caught the vibes for you." (Gives the layer a personality, and says nothing.)
- "Oops! Something went wrong. Let's gooo again!" (Vague, and performing youth instead of respecting it.)

## 5. Rules and risks

**Six things that never happen**

1. Nothing painted sits behind text that has to be read.
2. Nothing painted enters the thread unless a person sent it. No art, texture or character touches an AI surface.
3. A screen has at most one picture, and a screen with a picture has one line and one action.
4. No texture on controls. Buttons, bubbles, tabs and icons stay flat and exact. No decorative icons.
5. The role colours hold: violet in the interface means AI and nothing else, and pink is never a control state, a warning or an error.
6. The cat never speaks, and the register snap never happens a third time without a reason as good as the first two.

**Three risks for a UX portfolio piece**

| Risk | Why it matters here | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **The art upstages the thinking.** A reviewer sees a generated neon illustration pack and files the case under "dress-up". | The page's strength is five argued decisions. | Make the art a decision, in the page's own format (the sixth Decision block and the ladder). Cap it at six to eight finished images. Say in one line how they were made and directed. |
| **Two products on one page.** A hot painted world next to a true-black, exact interface, with real collisions: the art's violet against AI violet, cyan against Event_Teal, cobalt against Chat_Blue. | He is judged on systems, and "each color has one role" is the System headline. | The glass rule, shown. Art always sits in a frame with black around it. No art beside an AI card. Extend the token table with the art's roles. Check every pink against the destructive red and for contrast before it gets near a control. |
| **It reads as borrowed or cheap.** The look sits close to a well-known animated-film style, and generated characters drift: a different cat face per image, wrong hands, wrong instruments. | Serious creators spot a fake saxophone grip instantly. | Own what is specific: the fixed phone camera, the warm wood, the paint-to-print arc tied to product state. A model sheet for the cat, a colour script for the 39 days (night lamp, morning, evening, venue), and retouching on every hand and instrument. |

## 6. How the art develops from here

- **A fixed camera.** Every scene is shot from the phone, wherever it has been put down. It makes a series coherent and tells an art director exactly how to make the next one.
- **A colour script.** Five key frames that follow the story's clock: Day 1 11:14 PM, Day 3 8:02 AM, Day 19 7:05 PM, Day 24 6:10 PM, Day 39 8:58 PM. Same room, the light moves, and the last one is the venue.
- **Paint to print.** Early frames are loose and brushy; the launch frames are crisp, in register, halftone visible.
- **On the case-study page**, the page itself is outside the glass, so the room can live around the phones while every phone stays black. Follow the ladder there too: full painting for the hero and Launch night, a quiet crop (the desk, the light) for the working parts.

## 7. Open decisions (only the owner can make these)

1. **Does pink enter the interface tokens?** Recommended: yes, with one role, "Public" (the switch's on state, the formats, the link page), on large surfaces only. If it sits too close to the destructive red at small sizes, it stays brand-only.
2. **Does the cat have a public name, and can users send it as a sticker?** Recommended: named once by the crew in the demo thread, never by the product. Stickers later, a wordless set of eight, sent by people only.
3. **What happens to the dog?** Recommended: one image outside the product, no role.
4. **Does the mark change?** Recommended: keep the speech-bubble shape and reprint it as two plates in register on black. No cat in the app icon.
5. **Does the thread stay true black with no wallpaper?** Recommended: yes. The only visible change inside a thread is the poster styles (Splash, Ink, Paper) rebuilt in the press material.
6. **How is this presented on the portfolio?** Recommended: as today's version of a product that did not launch, with one plain line that the images are generated and art-directed by him.
