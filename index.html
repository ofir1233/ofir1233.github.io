Case study 01 · My initiative

Hear Design Lab

When AI tools arrived, I started a working replica of Hear on my own initiative, coded with Claude Code from a colleague's starting app. It runs on realistic demo data, and it's where I test designs before anything reaches development. It can also produce a demo tailored to a prospect.

Role
Initiator · design, coded with Claude Code
Scope
Working product replica
Built for
Testing designs · prospect demos
Stack
React · Vite · Storybook

The problem

Static mockups couldn't answer the real question: how does this design behave for this customer, with their data, at their scale?

What it is

A working copy of the product in React. It holds a demo environment for each customer, and a new one starts from a form with their website, a description and files. Key screens carry alternative versions I can switch between, to compare them for different kinds of customers.

Why it matters

Design decisions get made in a working product, with real interactions and realistic data, not in a slide deck. The same build serves design reviews and sales demos.

Case study 02 · The product

Thousands of calls, one clear answer

Hear reads every call, chat and ticket between a contact center's agents and its customers. My job is to make that much data feel calm: show what matters first, explain every number, and let people act from the same screen. People have to trust and audit what the AI concludes, so every score leads back to the calls behind it.

Role
Only designer and product person
Surfaces
Topics · Compliance · Agent evaluation · Actions
Domain
Contact-center AI
Focus
Explainable AI scores
Topics

What are customers calling about, and how is it going?

  • Boxes, circles and flow views
  • Hover breaks a score into its parameters
  • Sort daily scores by calls, lowest score or biggest drop
  • Click a parameter and the AI explains what drives it
Design choice: size shows call volume and color shows score, so problem areas stand out before anyone reads a number.
Compliance

Every agent, every criterion, readable at a glance

Any cell opens a popover that places that agent against the whole team and says what the next button will do before you press it. One click on Explore in Chat hands that cell to the AI chat to dig deeper.

Design choice: only problems get color. A full grid of agents and criteria stays quiet until something needs attention.
Agent evaluation

A score is useless unless it explains itself

See the trend, the distribution and every team on one screen, then ask the AI why a score moved.

Design choice: answers point to the exact calls behind a score, so people can check the AI instead of just trusting it.
Actions

From insight to done

Alerts, signals and evaluations become tasks with an owner and a priority. Inside a task the team ticks off action items, moves the status and @mentions a teammate, and every step lands in the activity log.

Design choice: every task keeps its source, and closed work keeps its reason, such as duplicate or escalated, so the team learns from it.
Newsroom · Data · Signals

The same data, told three ways

A newsroom that tells the month as a story and flags spikes against a baseline, a dense table to dig into, and signals that fire on their own.

Design choice: one set of data, three surfaces, each for a different person at a different moment.
Demos engine

A demo built for every prospect

To show a new prospect what Hear could do for their business, I designed a demos engine inside the Lab. It runs the product from a script: it types the prospect's questions, answers them on demo data and moves a camera across the screens, in English or Hebrew, with narration.

The aim: no generic deck. Every demo is ready to play live in a meeting or send as a video.

Map · Information architecture

How Hear is organized

I mapped Hear's surfaces: the product, the website and the sign-in and sign-up flows. The product map includes the admin layer: data retention, the anonymizer, 2FA, team permissions and impersonation. Pick a map, then open any area to see what lives inside it.

How I work · Process

One redesign, planned in eight phases

I planned Hear's redesign and rebrand as one program in eight phases, each with clear tasks and deliverables, from understanding the market to the workflows between design, development and marketing.

  1. 01

    Research

    Market and competitors, Hear's unique value, audiences, users and personas.

  2. 02

    Branding

    Essence and personality, identity, messaging, voice and tone, UX writing.

  3. 03

    Visual design

    Visual research, concepts, and crafting the identity.

  4. 04

    Website

    Information architecture, wireframes, design, video, development.

  5. 05

    Growth

    Sign-up, onboarding and setup, product walkthroughs.

  6. 06

    Product

    Current-state map, user types and paths, MVP scope, AI and LLM capabilities.

  7. 07

    UI design

    Product design, redesign flows, handoff and the design system.

  8. 08

    Process

    Principles, design-dev and marketing-design workflows, testing and iteration.
