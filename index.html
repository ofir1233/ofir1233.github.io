02
Case study 02

Product design

Hear reads every call, chat and ticket in a contact center. I make that data calm and explainable, built around how teams really use it: what matters comes first, and asked about one score, the AI names the calls behind it.

My job is to make that much data feel calm: show what matters first, explain every number, and let people act from the same screen. People have to trust and audit what the AI concludes, so an answer about one score names the calls behind it.

Role
Only designer and product person
Surfaces
Topics · Compliance · Agent evaluation · Actions · Newsroom · Data · Signals · Demos engine

Topics

The challengeEvery conversation in the contact center, and no way to see the whole picture or where problems hide.

In Ultra I put the key pain point first: open an AI chat about it in one click to understand the cause, then act on it with suggested signals.

  • Boxes, circles and flow views
  • Hover breaks a score into its parameters
  • Sort daily scores by calls, lowest score or biggest drop
  • Click a parameter and the AI explains what drives it

Design choice: size shows call volume and color shows score, so problem areas stand out before anyone reads a number.

Compliance

The challengeChecking every agent against every compliance rule, without drowning in numbers.

A heatmap of agents against criteria where only problems get color. Any cell puts that agent against the whole team, and one click hands it to the AI chat.

Any cell opens a popover that places that agent against the whole team and says what the next button will do before you press it. One click on Explore in Chat hands that cell to the AI chat to dig deeper.

Design choice: only problems get color. A full grid of agents and criteria stays quiet until something needs attention.

Agent evaluation

The challengeAn AI score is only useful if people can trust it.

V1 was my first version of the page. In my later versions, low periods turn coral, hover shows how many calls a score is based on, and an answer about one score names the calls that pulled it down.

I designed V1 with the score over time, the score per skill and the tables of teams and agents, then V2 to V4 + Filter. In this clip, a free question about a dip gets a general answer: the usual causes, and where to check.

Design choice: asked about one score, the answer points to the exact calls behind it, so people can check the AI instead of just trusting it.

Actions

The challengeInsights are only worth something if someone acts on them.

Alerts, signals and evaluations become tasks with a source, a priority and an assignee, and closed work keeps its reason.

Inside a task, the team ticks off action items, moves the status and @mentions a teammate, and the activity log records each change, such as Created, Assigned to and Moved to In progress.

Design choice: every task keeps its source, and closed work keeps its reason, such as duplicate or escalated, so the team learns from it.

Newsroom, data & signals

The challengeDifferent people need the same data at different moments: a summary, a deep dive, an alert.

One set of data, three surfaces: the month as a 75-second story, a table where the AI builds the filter, and signals that run on calls by themselves.

  • Newsroom tells the month as a 75-second story and flags spikes against a baseline
  • Data: describe the calls you want and the AI builds the filter, then open any call and ask about it
  • Signals: switch one on, or build a new one, with what to detect and the fields to pull from every call

Design choice: one set of data, three surfaces, each for a different person at a different moment.

Demos engine

The challengeShowing each prospect what Hear could do for their business.

A demos engine inside the Lab that drives its working screens from a script: it types the prospect's questions, the Lab answers on demo data, and a camera moves across the screens. In English or Hebrew, in a meeting or as a video.

To show a new prospect what Hear could do for their business, I designed a demos engine inside the Lab. It drives the Lab's working screens from a script: it types the prospect's questions, the Lab answers them on demo data, and a camera moves across the screens.

Each card in the library is a ready demo: play it in the Lab, or record a clean run.

The aim: no generic deck. Every demo is ready to play in a meeting or send as a video.

Redesign & rebrand

Process

I planned Hear's redesign and rebrand as one program in eight phases.

Each phase has clear tasks and deliverables, from understanding the market to the workflows between design, development and marketing.

  1. 01

    Research

    Market and competitors, Hear's unique value, audiences, users and personas.

  2. 02

    Branding

    Essence and personality, identity, messaging, voice and tone, UX writing.

  3. 03

    Visual design

    Visual research, concepts, and crafting the identity.

  4. 04

    Website

    Information architecture, wireframes, design, video, development.

  5. 05

    Growth

    Sign-up, onboarding and setup, product walkthroughs.

  6. 06

    Product

    Current-state map, user types and paths, MVP scope, AI and LLM capabilities.

  7. 07

    UI design

    Product design, redesign flows, handoff and the design system.

  8. 08

    Ways of working

    Principles, design-dev and marketing-design workflows, testing and iteration.

My solutions start from how customers really use the product, so they get value worth paying for with the least effort. That's part of why Hear keeps growing and winning customers.
