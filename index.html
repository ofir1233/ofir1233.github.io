02
Five product areas

Product design

Hear reads every call, chat and ticket in a contact center. I design the screens where a team finds the weak spot, sees why a score is low and opens the calls behind it.

They play live in Hear Design Lab, with demo data. One sample call runs through four of them.

Topics

The challengeCalls spread across many topics. Which ones are both big and scoring low?

I sized topics by calls and colored them by score, so the weak ones stand out before anyone reads a number.

One call, four screens stop 1 of 4

A sample call from Oct 13: a customer asks twice for a refund and is offered a voucher. In Topics it sits in Refunds & Credits, a topic that scores 36.

  • The Topics screen has boxes, circles and flow views
  • Hovering a topic shows the parameters behind its score
  • The daily score panel shows each topic’s last 30 days, its change and its score

Compliance

The challengeCompliance checks every contact-center agent against the center’s rules. Failures are easy to miss.

Only failing cells in the heatmap get color, and Ask AI names the calls that pulled a score down.

Only failing cells in the heatmap get color. Each cell’s popover compares the agent with the team, and from there Ask AI names the calls that pulled the score down.

The same call stop 2 of 4

Here the Oct 13 refund call explains Selma K.’s low score on one rule. Ask AI lists it first among the calls that pulled it down.

Each cell also shows its score as a number, so the grid never relies on color alone. Before anyone presses Ask AI which calls, the popover says what the button will do.

Agent evaluation

The challengeAgent evaluation gives each agent an AI score, useful only if people can check it.

A day far under the average turns coral. Opening it shows the score, the calls behind it and what fell.

I designed every version of this screen. In the latest, shown here, a day far under the average turns coral, and opening it shows the score, the calls behind it and what fell.

The same call skips this screen

Agent evaluation scores a whole day, not single calls, and its chart ends on Oct 12, the day before the call.

I kept two ways into the chat. From the sidebar, Ask AI takes the page and the project as context, so its answer is general. From a Compliance cell, Ask AI takes the agent, the criterion and the score with the question.

Actions

The challengeAlerts, signals and evaluations raise findings, but each one still needs an owner.

Each finding becomes a task with its source, a priority and an assignee. Closed work keeps its reason, such as Duplicate or Escalated.

The same call stop 3 of 4

The Oct 13 refund call raised an alert. The alert is now a task, High priority, assigned to Daniel Brooks.

In June 2026 I also designed AI Tasks, a coded prototype in the Lab. The AI proposes changes in groups, and a person decides on each group before anything is submitted.

Newsroom, Data & Signals

The challengeOne set of calls, three questions: what changed this month, which calls match, and what to flag without being asked.

Three surfaces for the same calls: a 75-second story, a filter built from plain words, and signals that classify calls on their own.

I designed three surfaces for that data. Newsroom plays the month as a 75-second story. Data builds a filter from plain words and shows it as chips people can read and remove. Signals classify calls on their own.

The same call stop 4 of 4

In Newsroom the Oct 13 refund call is one of six behind a story: refund asked twice, voucher offered.

  • Newsroom stories show their source and end with a line such as “See N calls”
  • Signals are AI or rule-based classifiers, and the form for a new one asks what to detect and which fields to pull from every call

Interactive maps

Information architecture

I mapped Hear’s product, website and sign-in flows, including the admin layer.

The admin layer covers data retention, the anonymizer, 2FA, team permissions and impersonation.

The product map runs from the dashboard to settings and billing, plus global controls such as the project switcher and the theme.

Redesign & rebrand

An eight-phase plan

I planned Hear’s redesign and rebrand as one program, from market research to the workflows between design, development and marketing.

  1. Market and competitors, Hear’s unique value proposition, audiences, users and personas.

  2. Essence and personality, identity, messaging, voice and tone, UX writing. See it above

  3. Visual research, concepts and the visual identity.

  4. Information architecture, wireframes, design, video, development. See it above

  5. Sign-up, onboarding and setup, product walkthroughs.

  6. Current-state map, user types and paths, MVP scope, AI and LLM capabilities. See it above

  7. Product design, redesign flows, handoff and the design system. See it above

  8. Principles, design-dev and marketing-design workflows, testing and iteration.

My solutions start from a deep understanding of how customers use the product, so getting value worth paying for takes them minimal effort. That’s part of why Hear keeps growing and winning customers.
